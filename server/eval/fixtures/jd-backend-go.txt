Senior Backend Engineer (Go)

About the role

A payments infrastructure company is looking for a Senior Backend Engineer to join the core ledger team. We process financial transactions for thousands of merchants and need engineers who treat correctness, auditability, and uptime as first-class requirements, not afterthoughts. You will design and operate the Go services that sit at the center of money movement: authorization, settlement, and reconciliation. This is a backend-heavy role with real production ownership from day one, including on-call rotation once ramped up.

What you will do

- Design, build, and maintain backend microservices in Go that process high-volume financial transactions with strict correctness and idempotency guarantees.
- Own the full lifecycle of services you build: API design, data modeling, testing, deployment, monitoring, and incident response.
- Collaborate with product and front-end teams to define clear API contracts (REST and gRPC) and evolve them without breaking downstream consumers.
- Improve observability across the ledger stack — structured logging, distributed tracing, and actionable alerting tied to SLOs.
- Participate in architecture reviews and write RFCs for significant system changes, especially anything touching data consistency or the money-movement path.
- Mentor mid-level and junior engineers through code review, pairing, and design discussions.
- Work with the platform team to run services reliably in a Kubernetes environment on AWS, including capacity planning and cost awareness.
- Drive down toil by automating repetitive operational work (schema migrations, backfills, reconciliation jobs).
- Participate in an on-call rotation (roughly one week in six) supporting production services you and your team own.

What we are looking for

- 5+ years of professional backend software engineering experience, with at least 3 years writing production Go.
- Deep understanding of concurrency in Go (goroutines, channels, sync primitives) and experience debugging real concurrency bugs in production.
- Strong grasp of distributed systems fundamentals: idempotency, exactly-once vs. at-least-once delivery, eventual consistency, and the tradeoffs between them.
- Experience with relational databases (PostgreSQL preferred) including schema design, indexing strategy, query optimization, and safe migrations at scale.
- Hands-on experience with asynchronous messaging systems such as Kafka, RabbitMQ, or SQS for decoupling services.
- Comfortable with containerization and orchestration (Docker, Kubernetes) and reading/writing basic Terraform for the infrastructure your services depend on.
- A track record of writing tests that actually catch regressions — unit, integration, and where relevant, property-based or fuzz tests for critical business logic.
- Experience with REST and/or gRPC API design, including versioning and backward compatibility strategies.
- Clear written and verbal communication; comfortable writing design docs and giving/receiving direct code review feedback.

Nice to have

- Experience in a regulated or high-compliance environment (payments, banking, healthcare) where auditability and data integrity are non-negotiable.
- Familiarity with double-entry accounting or ledger systems.
- Experience with observability tooling such as Prometheus, Grafana, Datadog, or OpenTelemetry.
- Contributions to open-source Go projects or libraries.
- Experience leading incident response and writing blameless postmortems.
- Exposure to event sourcing or CQRS patterns.

What we offer

Competitive salary and equity, fully remote-friendly with quarterly in-person team gatherings, a strong engineering culture that values write-ups over meetings, a generous learning and conference budget, and a team that takes on-call seriously enough to actually fix root causes instead of just paging people. We are a small, senior-leaning team — you will have real influence over how the system is built, not just where you're told to plug in.

How we work

We favor small, incremental changes with fast code review turnaround over big-bang rewrites. Every non-trivial change gets a short design doc before implementation. We run blameless postmortems after incidents and treat them as a source of engineering work, not blame. We believe strongly in testing the paths that touch money, and we would rather ship slower and correct than fast and wrong.
